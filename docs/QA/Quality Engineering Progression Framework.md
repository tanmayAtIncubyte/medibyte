# Quality Engineering Progression Framework

## Initial remarks

This is Incubyte's quality engineering progression framework, which has evolved from our engineering progression principles, and our guiding principles, with inspiration from other open-source frameworks (like Monzo) which are highly respected and referenced as best practice in career development.

This document is a perpetual work-in-progress and will never be "done".

As Incubyte's quality engineering practice matures, we may introduce specialized paths (e.g., performance, security, data quality) within this framework. This document establishes the foundational progression structure that will accommodate future specializations.

A few guiding pillars for quality engineering progression at Incubyte:

- We will focus on the impact an individual quality engineer can have on driving Incubyte and its client engagements forward, rather than specific behaviors.
- Scope is an additional measure for different levels of seniority. As Incubyte grows, so should you and the size or reach of the quality challenges you solve.
- We have aimed to consolidate behaviors down into essentials. This framework does not intend to be an exhaustive list of everything a quality engineer does or can do, but instead articulates the important distinctions between levels.
- Behaviors should be broad enough to factor in the numerous different ways a quality engineer can contribute effectively. For example: we should not list specific tasks such as "writes Playwright tests" but instead articulate the standards of quality contribution we should expect from engineers of different levels.
- We do not agree with typical levelling exercises or calculations. Attempting to boil performance and progression down to numbers and equations leaves little room for the different ways quality engineers can add value. Instead, we rely on TL or EMs to clearly articulate how a quality engineer is meeting the scope and impact of each level.
- This articulation is taken to a group of TLs and EMs for calibration across the company so that we set a common bar across the company leaving less scope for personal interpretations of the levels.
- Our framework should focus on the practice of quality engineering itself; it should not matter which specific testing tool or framework you choose to use.
- Levels are cumulative. Particularly within each behavioral theme or category, we expect quality engineers to be demonstrating the contents of previous levels in addition to their own level.
- We evaluate each behavior on the 3 dimensional axis of **Initiative**, **Closure**, **Consistency**.

## Impact

Impact is the main driver for progression at Incubyte. Put simply, impact measures your sphere of influence and individual input on our success, through contributing to Incubyte's mission and goals. The client project you work in may mean you can have an impact in different ways—for instance, establishing quality standards for a pre-product founder's MVP, improving test coverage in an enterprise legacy system, enabling a scale-up to ship confidently at speed, or more. You may also have an impact in other indirect ways, for example, improving testing practices or helping others grow.

Focusing on impact enables us to reward quality engineers who "get quality done" without needing to perform specific routines. We believe impact results from the combination of building quality engineering skill, demonstrating it, learning from that experience over time, and coupling it with important behaviors and core skills.

### Excellence here looks like

- You "get quality done". You work for the team and are regarded as a highly effective contributor by your peers.
- The quality work you choose to do drives team, client and business goals forward (relevant to the scope of your role).
- You proactively identify opportunities to raise the quality bar within engineering, take the initiative to move them forward, and take them to closure.
- You prevent defects through early involvement, enable fast delivery through efficient testing, and advocate for quality throughout the SDLC.
- You do all of the above repeatedly and consistently.

## Craft & Quality Engineering Skills

This aspect is about how you apply your quality engineering skills and hone your craft. It documents the types of behavior that measure the effectiveness of a quality engineer's contribution at different levels of complexity and ambiguity. Think of this as "how you do your quality work".

Behaviors in this area should not be at odds with doing what is most impactful for your team and Incubyte. Instead, this category focuses on measuring quality contribution via test effectiveness, coverage appropriateness, defect prevention, risk assessment, and ability to design and critique test strategies. Your quality engineering skills will gradually scale and improve with experience.

### Excellence here looks like

- You are on your way to mastery of practices that help you ensure well-crafted, reliable software through effective quality engineering.
- Your tests and quality contributions are regarded as high quality by your peers and those at higher levels in the framework.
- You can efficiently design test strategies to address risk and solve client quality challenges, resolving ambiguity at both technical and product levels.
- Your tests are maintainable, reliable, well-designed, and scale to meet customer growth expectations—designed for clarity and purpose, not just coverage metrics.
- You are continuously delivering quality feedback fast and frequently.
- You balance testing types appropriately based on context and risk.
- You think like an adversary but act like an advocate—finding issues while partnering with the team on solutions.

## Behaviors

Behaviors are a collection of expectations and development areas for quality engineers that do not fall into the categories of craft and quality engineering skills, or impact. They encompass a range of core skills that we expect quality engineers at Incubyte to embody.

Through your behavior, you set an example to those around you. By modelling great behaviors, you lead by example, and by being self-aware you know when you are setting a great example. Your behaviors show that you're growing good habits, and cultivating qualities that make you an excellent quality engineer, rather than unintentionally doing the "right thing" by luck.

These behaviors are even more critical in making remote work successful. As a baseline, you consistently model remote-first best practices: keeping video on in meetings, creating predictability around your availability, being responsive and approachable to foster collaboration. You not only practice these yourself but also set expectations and hold others respectfully accountable, regardless of their seniority, so the team can do its best work.

### Excellence here looks like

- You take ownership of quality outcomes and follow through without excuses.
- You collaborate openly, seek input, and create clarity for your team and clients.
- You learn continuously, share knowledge, and help others grow.
- You give and act on feedback constructively, raising the bar for everyone.
- You build trust by acting with integrity and consistency.
- You put client and team success at the center of your decisions.
- You advocate for quality without being a blocker, champion testability in design discussions, and bridge technical and business perspectives effectively.

## Usage of the progression framework

### How the framework should be used

The purpose of this document is to guide conversations around performance and progression between leads and quality engineers. This articulates the expectations of each level, the level of impact we expect individuals to bring to quality challenges, and the manner in which quality engineers should be conducting their work. This document is not designed to be useful as a standalone tool.

> This is a compass, not a GPS.

Progression from one level to the next will be a conversation between the individual and their lead. To reiterate, this should not be seen as a checkbox exercise. Some individuals may move up a level without having experienced certain skills or behaviors, whilst also giving confidence that they're meeting the level above. In other situations a controlling weakness may prevent the individual moving forwards. The framework does not intend to codify every possible situation, instead laying out expectations in broad strokes.

For any promotion cases, leads and quality engineers will together need to be able to articulate clearly how the quality engineer has demonstrated the required impact for the level they are being promoted to, and should have demonstrated experience in the related quality engineering skill and behaviors in order to have that impact.

We will document the promotions process separately.

### How the framework should not be used

This framework should not be used as a checklist or to come up with some mathematical formula. Different behaviors are important in different ways, and attempting to create algorithms or formulae to support conversations naively assumes that each behavior is important in the same way.

This framework is not intended to compare individuals against one another. It is a tool for understanding and enabling personal growth, not for ranking or distributing people along a curve. We do not aim for a bell curve, nor do we expect a fixed distribution of performance across levels. Each person's progression should be measured against their own journey, impact, and scope — not someone else's.

## Framework

The levels below represent a career's worth of progression through quality engineering at Incubyte.

Levels are broadly cumulative, each one building on what comes before.

## Level Definitions

---

### Associate Test Crafter (Level 1)

**Scope:** Task

A test crafter at the start of their career, working on individual test scenarios under close guidance. Expected to progress to "Test Crafter I" within 1 year.

#### Impact

- Contributes to team goals through executing well-defined testing tasks.
- Reports defects clearly with reproducible steps.
- Begins shifting left by participating early in refinement sessions.

#### Technical Skills

- Has foundational grasp of testing principles, applying concepts under guidance.
- Executes existing test suites to ensure no regressions occurred.
- Writes simple test cases under guidance, documenting results with evidence.
- Performs exploratory testing sessions, documenting findings clearly.
- Learns team's testing tools and follows established patterns.
- Understands test pyramid basics.

#### Behaviors

- Focused on fast personal learning; asks questions.
- Actively seeks feedback and responds with prompt action.
- Engages fully and proactively in team practices.
- Proactively communicates status, blockers, and assistance needed.
- Approaches uncertainty with curiosity.
- Understands the product and client domain.
- Begins building quality mindset by questioning assumptions and identifying edge cases.
- Represents Incubyte authentically as a growing, AI-augmented quality engineer.

---

### Test Crafter I (Level 2)

**Scope:** Task

A quality engineer with high potential, developing the fundamentals needed to be successful in their team. Working on well-defined testing tasks with growing independence. Expected to progress to "Test Crafter II" within 1.5 years at Incubyte.

#### Impact

- Delivers well-defined tasks on time, collaborating with experienced engineers.
- Designs test suites and maintains reliability with growing independence.
- Shifts left by identifying testability concerns and raising quality risks early.

#### Technical Skills

- Demonstrates growing fluency with team's testing stack and established patterns.
- Designs test cases using structured techniques.
- Plans and executes charter-based exploratory testing within defined charters.
- Writes basic automation scripts independently following team frameworks.
- Develops working knowledge of system architecture, key dependencies, API testing basics, SQL fundamentals, and accessibility awareness.
- Learns to balance verification depth with execution speed and maintenance.
- Understands test pyramid concept and CI/CD pipeline basics.
- Participates in peer reviews of test cases and automation scripts.

#### Behaviors

- Reflects growth by consistently adopting best practices from reviews and pair testing sessions.
- Gives constructive, specific feedback; receives feedback openly and implements improvements.
- Builds trust by delivering commitments reliably and communicating proactively about risks.
- Connects testing work to user stories, understanding the "why" behind each test.
- Embraces ambiguity with solution-oriented mindset; raises problems with possible paths forward.
- Engages in Incubyte's growth through guild activities and knowledge sharing.

---

### Test Crafter II (Level 3)

**Scope:** Feature

A productive and reliable quality engineer working effectively within a team. Solid individual contributor independently owning quality for complete features from conception to release. Expected to progress to "Test Crafter III" within 2 years.

#### Impact

- Owns quality strategy and test coverage for entire features end-to-end.
- Designs test strategies with risk-based decisions on depth and breadth.
- Champions shift-left by influencing feature design and surfacing quality risks early.
- Breaks down complex features into testable slices for incremental delivery.

#### Technical Skills

- Demonstrates strong quality judgment at feature level.
- Applies advanced test design techniques using heuristics.
- Designs test architectures with appropriate abstractions, test data strategies, and reusable components.
- Refactors test suites to improve maintainability and design.
- Understands and optimizes test distribution across system layers, eliminating anti-patterns.
- Connects testing work to DORA metrics and DevEx, identifying feedback bottlenecks.
- Learns performance, security, and accessibility testing with guidance.
- Assesses and verifies non-functional aspects where applicable.
- Participates actively in Three Amigos sessions, shaping acceptance criteria and testing approaches.

#### Behaviors

- Demonstrates strong learning mindset by experimenting with new techniques and sharing insights.
- Holds peers accountable with clear, kind feedback on test approaches and quality concerns.
- Collaborates proactively with developers on testability.
- Brings structure to ambiguous testing challenges.
- Has MVP mindset; delivers small slices frequently.
- Champions testability in feature discussions.
- Takes initiative to strengthen team quality practices and mentor juniors.

### Test Crafter III (Level 4)

**Scope:** Initiative / POD

A strong individual contributor and emerging quality leader responsible for quality strategy across an entire pod. Capable of mentoring others, leading quality discussions, and driving quality improvements within their pod, with collaboration primarily focused at the pod level.

At this level, quality engineers often develop deeper expertise in either automation or manual testing approaches based on context, engagement needs, and personal growth trajectory. Both paths are equally valued and essential to Incubyte's quality engineering excellence. Engineers are expected to maintain competency in both areas while deepening mastery in one.

#### Impact

- Owns and delivers a quality-first strategy for all pod features, maintaining consistent standards.
- Leads quality discussions in planning sessions, providing risk assessments and test effort estimates.
- Independently manages production quality incidents, including triage and root-cause analysis.
- Influences design and architecture decisions to improve testability and reliability.
- Mentors engineers and drives pod-wide quality improvements across tools and practices.

#### Technical Skills

**Automation**

- Architects maintainable automation frameworks scaling across multiple system layers.
- Defines automation conventions and quality gates ensuring consistency.
- Integrates multiple automated verification approaches across system layers.
- Optimizes test execution for speed and reliability.
- Leads automation debt reduction and suite maintainability improvements.
- Mentors junior engineers on automation best practices and debugging approaches.

**Manual**

- Designs structured exploratory testing campaigns using charters, personas, and heuristics.
- Leads manual testing for complex scenarios impractical to automate.
- Conducts risk-based testing targeting high-uncertainty areas requiring human judgment.
- Designs manual test strategies for usability, accessibility, and cross-device compatibility.
- Leads defect triage and root cause analysis, identifying patterns and systemic issues.
- Balances manual testing with automation investment based on context and ROI.

**Common**

- Designs verification strategies for initiatives, balancing coverage, speed, and maintenance.
- Shapes pod testing practices through clear guidelines and standards.
- Identifies quality bottlenecks using DORA/DevEx principles to reduce feedback time.
- Evaluates emerging testing tools and AI-augmented approaches for pod efficiency.
- Guides prioritization through risk analysis, balancing quality with delivery speed.
- Balances bug fixes with strategic test debt reduction.

#### Behaviors

- Promotes quality-first culture within the pod.
- Drives measurable quality improvements (flakiness reduction, faster CI/CD feedback).
- Uses quality metrics to surface trends and business impact.
- Communicates quality risks clearly to technical and non-technical stakeholders.
- Brings clarity and resilience in ambiguous situations.
- Aligns quality strategy with product goals and team KPIs.
- Partners with developers to improve testability and set quality standards.

**Automation**

- Aligns automation approaches across teams with shared practices.

- Establishes quality gates and CI/CD best practices for the project.
- Debugs complex automation and product issues across systems.

**Manual**

- Orchestrates project-wide exploratory testing strategies.
- Leads manual testing for high-risk scenarios across pods.
- Harmonizes exploratory testing approaches across the project.
- Drives post-incident learnings to improve quality processes.

**Common**

- Leads multiple teams to strong quality outcomes.
- Recognized for impactful quality contributions across projects.
- Inspires others in quality craft and technical mastery.
- Designs project-wide verification strategies integrating all testing approaches.
- Establishes and monitors project-wide quality metrics.
- Evaluates and introduces testing tools benefiting the project.

#### Behaviors

- Grooms others in quality practices to scale leadership.
- Leads quality improvement initiatives balancing rigor and pragmatism.
- Provides trend-based feedback on quality metrics to stakeholders.
- Handles difficult quality trade-off conversations with clients and leadership.
- Leads calmly in crises; creates safety for experimentation.
- Provides creative, business-aligned quality solutions.
- Elevates Incubyte's quality brand through ideas and practices.

## Quality Engineering at Incubyte: Core Principles

### 1. Testing is not just finding bugs — it's about:

- Risk assessment and mitigation
- Quality advocacy throughout SDLC
- Building testable systems
- Fast, reliable feedback loops
- Enabling confident, rapid delivery

### 2. Balance is critical:

- Automated vs. Manual vs. Exploratory
- Speed vs. Thoroughness
- Coverage metrics vs. Confidence
- Prevention vs. Detection
- Quality rigor vs. Delivery pragmatism

### 3. Shift-left, but don't ignore shift-right:

- Early involvement in design and testability discussions
- Production monitoring and observability
- Learning from production issues to improve testing strategies
- Quality feedback at every stage

### 4. Quality is a team sport:

- Enable developers to test effectively
- Coach testability practices across the team
- Build quality culture, not quality police
- Partner on solutions, not just report problems
- Think like an adversary, act like an advocate
